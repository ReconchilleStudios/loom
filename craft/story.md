# Story

How a Loom film is built from the inside: the structures that worked, what each length can carry, the hook, the peak, the close, and the taste the line has settled on.

## What a length can carry

**30 seconds: one idea, one turn.** In the demos:

| Part | Time | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–3 s | Motion in the first frame, and a question or a number the viewer reads while seeing it |
| Problem | to ~5 s | The world without the product, felt rather than explained |
| Turn | ~5 s | The problem becomes the product, in one physical move (a stack flips into the logo card) |
| Proof | 5–26 s | Two or three moments that move, each on its own words |
| Close | 26–30 s | The run's end card |

**Two minutes: a story.** A day, a deal followed through, a result rewound. Chapters follow the music's sections. The peak line lands at about 60–70 %, and there is a breath of room before the close. A two-minute film shows the product solving a real day; it does not explain every feature.

## Structures

Each concept starts from a different one (`stages/3-room.md`). Each was used by a Loom film:

- **The collapse.** Many tools, tabs or steps pile up and accelerate, then stack into the one product. *30A "Twelve Places":* twelve windows land faster and faster under a super counting "12 different places", then stack, and the pile flips over into the logo card.
- **The wrong signal.** The question everyone answers wrong, then the right signal. *30B "Who Sells More":* 1.2M followers against 184K, and the smaller creator outsells the bigger one eight to one.
- **A day.** One person's working day, solved scene by scene. *2:00A "Monday at Brightfold":* from the command center to the approved video and what it sold, under a day clock.
- **The rewind.** Open on the result, rewind to the morning it started, play forward to the next decision. *2:00B "Rewind":* a scrubber and a day counter, with the score reversed and pitched up through the rewind.
- **One out of many.** A counter runs over a wall of items, then the film follows one of them through the product. *"One of 155,195."*
- **The user's voice.** The person the product serves tells it, and the product answers in its own sentences. *"Nobody Emailed."*
- **The question answered by the last frame.** A question in the brand's own type, answered by the film's final image.
- **Before and after,** **the count,** **the object's journey:** for when none of the above fits.

## The hook

- The first frame moves. No logo, no fade from black, no title card.
- The viewer reads something while seeing it: a number that grows, a question, a contradiction.
- Within two seconds, something on screen makes the viewer ask what happens next.

## The peak

Every film has one line or image it exists for: often the brief's must-say line ("Follower count tells you who has an audience. / Sales DNA tells you who can move a customer."). It gets its own stage (TYPE, or a split), the score's biggest change of register (`craft/score.md`), and time to be read.

## Craft that carries

- **Every action lands on a spoken word.** "On 'audience' the slider moves" is how a screenplay is written.
- **A callback:** the same object opens and closes the middle of the film. In 30A the purple logo card appears at 0:05 and again at 0:25.
- **A signature device** per version that no other version uses, and **one series move** they all share.
- **Show the product moving.** Every product beat is an ask and a landing: something is asked of the product, and the result arrives and is held long enough to read. Static screens with nice cuts are not a product film.
- **Supers say what the picture does not.** They never repeat a heading on screen.
- **On-screen copy:** the film's language, no em or en dashes (a period or a comma instead), numbers exactly as in `world.md`.

## The end card

One system for the whole run, built once in each version, 4–6 s. The demos' system:

1. Four word cards (MATCH. CREATE. SELL. MEASURE.) land on four beats, each in one of the brand's colours.
2. They slide up into one row as the wordmark lands on the music's hit, followed by the tagline and the URL.
3. The CTA button lands and the cursor presses it. That is the last use of the accent colour.
4. The last frame holds for 12 frames.

A run's end card comes from its own brand, in the same spirit: the position in words, the mark, one action.

## House taste

Defaults the line has settled on. The bible keeps them unless this film has a reason not to.

- **Software is shown where it is used.** World footage for a SaaS film is offices, hands on keyboards and phones, and screens. It is not the sample customer's trade: a coffee roaster's beans are sample data, and they belong inside the product's screens.
- **Near the end, a still wakes into motion.** A generated still that comes alive (a hold, then easing to 1× on a music hit) is a stronger payoff than a stock intercut.
- **The score never stops,** and nothing dips to black or flashes white. The only darkness is darkness in the story.
- **Generated outputs are never retouched.** A face with a defect is regenerated or cut.

## Every screenplay ends with

A "What makes it great" list of 4–6 lines, specific to the version, which QC reads before the render. For 30A it was:

- the counter in the opening super is the hook;
- the logo card at 0:05 and 0:25 is the same object;
- every press lands on a word;
- the accent colour appears only where the UI has it;
- nothing is ever static.
